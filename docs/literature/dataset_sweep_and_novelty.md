Screening is not full review, proof verification or an exhaustive literature search.

A paper's contribution below is what it proposes or investigates. It is not an independently certified first-ever claim. “Not established in reviewed passages” means that the review did not establish that protocol; it is not proof that no section, supplement or related paper covers it.

## What this changes for our study

Our completed dense D curves and traceable artifacts are useful baseline evidence. More labels improving a frozen classifier, cached linear probing, communication with pretrained embeddings, and connecting geometry to sample scaling each have prior work. None of these broad ideas should be presented as our novel contribution.

A more focused candidate asks whether channel width/noise merely shift an observed downstream learning curve or change its shape, and whether a small pilot predicts larger-D outcomes under an explicit training/power policy. This is a **candidate empirical distinction**, not certified worldwide novelty. The [research question and proposed protocol](../research_question.md) specify falsifiable alternatives and the controls needed to make a useful answer possible.

The literature does not supply one universally correct D grid. Our nesting/repetition choices have precedents, while our early-stopped fixed recipe does not provide matched compute. Another paper's pretraining D or examples-seen axis should never be relabeled as our downstream-label D.

The next literature work should follow citations and forward citations for P14/P16/P17/P18 and the closest communication papers, inspect supplements and released code, and ask the supervisor to challenge the exact gap statement. Sparse or empty search results are not proof of originality.
